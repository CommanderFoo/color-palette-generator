Color Harmonies

Analogous

Colors that sit next to each other on the color wheel. Creates a serene, comfortable design with low contrast. Great for nature themes.

Monochromatic

Different shades, tints, and tones of a single hue. Creates a cohesive, elegant look with subtle variations.

Triadic

Three colors equally spaced on the color wheel (120° apart). Offers vibrant contrast while maintaining balance.

Tetradic

Four colors forming a rectangle on the wheel (90° apart). Rich color scheme that works best with one dominant color.

Complementary

Two colors directly opposite each other on the wheel. Creates high contrast and visual tension—great for making elements pop.

Split Complementary

A base color plus the two colors adjacent to its complement. Strong contrast with less tension than pure complementary.

Accessibility Guide

WCAG Contrast Requirements

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define minimum contrast ratios to ensure text is readable for people with low vision.

AAA (7:1+) Enhanced contrast. Best for body text and critical UI elements.
AA (4.5:1+) Minimum for normal text (14pt). Required for WCAG 2.1 Level AA.
Fail (<4.5:1) Insufficient contrast. Text may be hard to read for many users.

Why Colors Fail

Colors fail when there isn't enough luminance difference between foreground and background. Common issues:

  • Light on light — Pastel text on white backgrounds
  • Dark on dark — Dark gray text on black backgrounds
  • Similar hues — Colors that look different but have similar brightness

Color-Blind Simulations

About 8% of men and 0.5% of women have some form of color vision deficiency:

  • Protanopia — Reduced sensitivity to red light
  • Deuteranopia — Reduced sensitivity to green light
  • Tritanopia — Reduced sensitivity to blue light (rare)

Use simulations to ensure your palette is distinguishable for all users.
